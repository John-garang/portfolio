CNN Academy

CNN Academy Voices of the Global South Climate Storytelling Fellowship

July – November 2025 Global (Online)

The CNN Academy Voices of the Global South Climate Storytelling Fellowship is a competitive journalism program designed to support emerging storytellers from the Global South in producing impactful climate narratives through ethical, evidence-based, and human-centered reporting. The fellowship focuses on amplifying local perspectives and ensuring that communities most connected to climate challenges are represented in global climate conversations.

The fellowship began with an intensive online curriculum delivered through the CNN Academy Learning Hub. I successfully completed interactive modules covering journalism ethics, breaking news reporting, digital storytelling, writing for online audiences, social storytelling, field reporting, television scriptwriting, mobile journalism, mobile editing using LumaFusion, Open Source Intelligence (OSINT), artificial intelligence in journalism, health reporting, and newsroom workflows. These courses strengthened my editorial judgment, research and verification skills, interviewing techniques, multimedia production abilities, and capacity to communicate complex issues through engaging storytelling.

For my final pitch, I developed a story exploring how writers and poets in South Sudan and the wider Global South are using creative spaces as platforms for climate advocacy — examining how a new generation of literary voices is using poetry, storytelling, and written expression to raise awareness about climate challenges, encourage environmental responsibility, and inspire communities to take part in climate mitigation efforts. Although my story was not selected for the in-person newsroom simulation in Abu Dhabi, the pitching process strengthened my ability to develop climate-focused narratives and approach storytelling with a deeper understanding of the relationship between people, culture, and the environment. As a CNN Academy Fellow, I now have lifetime access to the CNN Academy Learning Hub.

Climate Journalism Multimedia Storytelling Digital Communications OSINT Mobile Journalism Global South Climate Advocacy
"The most powerful climate stories are not only about the environment, but about the people using their voices to protect its future."

UNLEASH

UNLEASH Global Innovation Lab

December 2–9, 2023 Kigali, Rwanda

Selected on a full scholarship as one of approximately 1,000 young innovators from around the world, I participated in the UNLEASH Global Innovation Lab in Kigali, Rwanda — one of the world's most prestigious platforms for youth-led innovation aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Using UNLEASH's internationally recognised design thinking and innovation methodology, participants collaborated in diverse teams to develop scalable, locally grounded solutions to global challenges.

Working alongside teammates from The Gambia, Chile, the United Kingdom, Pakistan, and Cambodia, our team created PappyBags — a sustainable innovation that transforms papyrus reeds growing along the River Nile in South Sudan into biodegradable shopping bags as an alternative to single-use plastics. Our solution advanced to the knockout stage before the finals, demonstrating the viability of using local natural resources to address climate innovation and environmental sustainability challenges.

Tilting Futures (formerly Global Citizen Year)

Take Action Lab

March – June 2024 Cape Town, Western Cape, South Africa

The Take Action Lab is a highly selective international leadership and global citizenship program for young adults aged 17–22. I attended the program on a full scholarship as part of a cohort of 52 young leaders representing 37 countries. Living and learning alongside peers from Nigeria, Ethiopia, Nepal, Bangladesh, Ukraine, Chile, and other parts of the world expanded my understanding of cross-cultural communication, community development, and the role of young people in addressing global challenges.

My apprenticeship journey began with Tshintsha Amakhaya before I joined the Surplus People Project (SPP) as a Multimedia Apprentice. Founded in 1979, SPP is a South African social justice organisation advancing food sovereignty, agroecology, land rights, and human rights for rural communities. During my three months with SPP, I travelled across the Western, Northern, Eastern Cape, and Gauteng provinces documenting grassroots initiatives through photography, videography, graphic design, and strategic communications. My work took me to Mamre, Wolseley, Springbok, Loeriesfontein, Namakwa Vanrhynsdorp, and Boontjiesrivier — where I facilitated a three-day social media training workshop for SAFE Youth (Sovereign Agro-Ecology Food Empowerment).

I also travelled to Gauteng Province, including Johannesburg, to cover the National Indaba Conference on behalf of Tshintsha Amakhaya, and visited Pretoria before returning to Cape Town. I concluded my apprenticeship by travelling to Paarl in the Eastern Cape to document a Food Sovereignty workshop. Through these experiences, I strengthened my skills in multimedia storytelling, campaign communication, video production, graphic design, and digital strategy while learning directly from rural communities working on food systems, environmental sustainability, and social justice.

Human Rights Agroecology Food Sovereignty Strategic Communications Community Development Global Leadership Gauteng South Africa

U.S. Department of State · RELO · TUSSAA

Eastern Africa English for Business & Entrepreneurship Hackathon (E4BE Hackathon)

February – May 2026 Arusha, Tanzania 14 Countries

The Eastern Africa English for Business & Entrepreneurship Hackathon (E4BE Hackathon) is a premier regional initiative funded by the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs via the Regional English Language Office (RELO), implemented in partnership with the Tanzania U.S. State Alumni Association (TUSSAA). Designed for elite young African changemakers and U.S. government exchange alumni across 14 countries, the program focuses on advanced business communication, cross-border economic collaboration, and pitching market-driven solutions. I successfully completed the intensive Virtual Massive Open Online Course (MOOC) phase before advancing as one of the top 90 regional finalists selected to form cross-border innovation teams and compete at the main pitch event in Arusha.

During the hackathon, my team and I developed MedLink — a low-bandwidth teleconsultation app engineered as a "crisis-first" digital health network. Built specifically to tackle care continuity failure in conflict environments like Sudan, the platform bridges the gap between stranded patients and displaced medical professionals using an app and structured WhatsApp intake. Applying customer-centered design, lean startup methodologies, and technical pitch mechanics cultivated during the program, we designed an agile tiered pricing matrix ($2–$15) tailored for crisis economies while embedding a self-sustaining 25% gross margin model to clear doctor payout overheads. This high-stakes environment significantly accelerated my competencies in corporate pitching, cross-border partner alignment, international venture scaling, and business English communication.

Healthcare Technology Crisis Innovation Strategy Cross-Border Collaboration Pitching & Venture Capital Business Communication Lean Business Modeling
"Every great venture starts by turning a modern connection into a lifeline for those who need it most."

Google Africa Headquarters · African Leadership University (ALU)

Nairobi Nexus Immersive Hackathon

June 2025 Nairobi, Kenya Innovation Team Lead & Presenter

As part of the African Leadership University (ALU) Nairobi Nexus immersive learning experience, I participated in an intensive, high-pressure hackathon hosted at the Google Africa Headquarters in Nairobi. Selected to pitch in front of industry tech leaders, my team was challenged to conceptualize, build, and present a functional product prototype within a strict 90-minute limit utilizing Google Firebase architecture. Leveraging my training in entrepreneurial leadership and venture design, I took the lead on strategic problem identification, user experience mapping, solution wireframing, and pitch deck architecture.

During the sprint, my team developed SimuSalama — a fraud-prevention web app designed to combat the rising crisis of mobile money scams across East Africa. The platform empowers users by allowing them to report suspicious transactions, instantly cross-reference numbers against a crowdsourced fraud database, receive real-time fraud likelihood scores, and access localized prevention tips. While technical teammates executed the live database demo, I orchestrated the presentation flow and delivered the high-stakes opening pitch. Despite an unexpected high-pressure pause mid-presentation, I demonstrated resilience, adapted on the fly, and coordinated a seamless transition to the live demo. The experience solidified my competencies in rapid prototyping, agile product design, rapid problem-solving, and crisis communication under tight institutional deadlines.

Rapid Prototyping Product Strategy & UX Agile Team Leadership Crisis Communication FinTech Security Solutions Entrepreneurial Leadership

YALI East Africa Regional Leadership Centre

November – December 2024 Nairobi, Kenya (Virtual) Cohort 60

The Young African Leaders Initiative (YALI) East Africa Regional Leadership Centre is one of the U.S. Department of State's flagship leadership development programs for emerging African leaders, funded by USAID and implemented by Kenyatta University. I participated virtually in the Business and Entrepreneurship Track as part of Cohort 60, joining young innovators from across East Africa to strengthen my entrepreneurial, leadership, and business development skills.

During the program, I developed an AI-powered agricultural venture that analyses crop health through image recognition and provides underserved farmers with disease diagnosis and treatment recommendations — applying innovation and entrepreneurship to address food security challenges across the region. The experience significantly strengthened my understanding of customer-centred design, business modelling, innovation strategy, and leadership. Becoming a YALI alumnus connected me to one of Africa's largest youth leadership networks, opening doors to future opportunities in entrepreneurship, international programs, and community development.
