Jonglei's Tragedy Is Intellectual

John GarangOctober 14, 2025 · 1 min read

Jonglei's tragedy isn't merely territorial, as many may perceive it.

It is intellectual.

The very people who understand the land, from contours to histories and agreements of settlement to boundaries, are excluded from the online drama by illiteracy. Meanwhile, the tantrum wave of young boys raised in the relative comfort of Equatoria, refugee camps, and East African suburbs have become the loudest claimants and most reckless provocateurs.

The deeper part of this crisis is that both these illiterates and the self-proclaimed intellects lack the critical faculties needed to interrogate the rumors traded on dining tables and in tea joints. Please — your brother who left the village at the age of three is neither a credible nor citable source on ancestral geography.

What do you mean when you open your mouth from Kakuma and shout, "We will fight over this land"?

If your so-called education carries substance, anything at all, it shouldn't manifest as performative outrage. True education must facilitate dialogue. Intellects don't shout or threaten; they reason and consult. Intellects fight with knowledge. Intellects recite from memory; they name every stream and every border post. True wisdom lies in knowing both what and when yours is to speak.

So, wait, when it's time to fight over Ayilo or Kalobeyei, we can leave it to you. You'd not just be a settler; you'd be an expert!
