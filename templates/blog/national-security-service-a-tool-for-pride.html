National Security Service: A Tool for Pride?

John GarangMay 1, 2026 · 6 min read
johngarang.com

There is a painful story in Sherkat, and now in Bor.

You'd find some stupid young man, half-trained and fully armed, parading through the streets with a rifle slung across his shoulders, announcing himself as national security. They think it's sassy, and that every kid would dream of being like them. And partially, they are right, because we are nursing a foolish generation. But come to think of it, what's national about that? Do you see any security? I don't. I just see performance from untrained militia looking for validation, and sadly, a bad one. It's not their ego or arrogance that disturbs me, but rather their belief that a security institution is genuinely supposed to look like that.

If you have watched an intelligence series, let alone studied intelligence systems, you'll know one basic truth: visibility is failure. But when you recruit your NSS officers for loyalty instead of competence, some of whom have never seen a TV screen, what do you expect? Of course they'll advertise themselves like kids showing off their Christmas clothes. Real National Security Service personnel must be invisible; not even their own family should know their occupation. In functional states, once your cover is blown, the operation is compromised, your work collapses, and you are either dismissed from service or dispatched to another region where you'll remain anonymous. Real intelligence operates with discretion, under high discipline and analytical skills. They don't make noise. They don't ask, "itabaref ana munu?" And they don't carry big guns around like Gucci chest bags. Our NSS is not a national security institution. Instead of gathering intelligence for the nation's safety, they are busy intimidating civilians while collecting propaganda from Facebook pages, seasoning it with false ingredients, and presenting it to their masters. They brandish guns in public, ride in convoys, and even demand recognition. It remains a mystery whether they understand the definition of "intelligence."

Real intelligence does counterterrorism, espionage, or internal threat analysis. They don't babysit or gatekeep failed regimes or nurse political egos. They chase and analyze national threats, not political opinions. Real intelligence blends into crowds. Not even the police or the army should know who a security agent is, and they don't even wear uniform; they live in the simplest ways. You'll find them selling tomatoes in the market, working as guards or cleaners at banks, conductors at taxi parks, members of church groups, gang members, and businesspeople. They look like ordinary people because they are supposed to listen, observe, and gather evidence, not perform masculinity in the middle of a community they are supposed to monitor. In other countries, they even go undercover as criminals to infiltrate networks, but our NSS poses for pictures in Hai Jalaba studios to look like heroes.

And the truth is, intelligence agencies act on evidence that proves beyond reasonable doubt that a suspect is most likely associated with specific claims. Intelligence isn't about partisan rumors like "this one criticized the government on Facebook." Proper intelligence takes months or years of documenting evidence, verified information, long-term observation, and clear legal thresholds. And if you're wondering what the NSS smokes when they arrest civilians, me too. Intelligence officers do not, and should not, arrest suspects. Once they've done their digging, they hand over the evidence to the police and CID, who do the dirty job. Intelligence officers may even arrive at the arrest scene later and act surprised, sometimes staging resistance to their "friend's" arrest. Sadly, for the NSS, they drink all day, go on 64 Tribes Press, collect whatever rubbish those admins post, then season it with a few lies and call for backup jeeps to come and arrest innocent civilians. Furthermore, they have no equipment for their work. Their only equipment is guns and uniforms, which signal power but have no purpose or competence.

You know what's even more absurd?

Every hotel or lodge, by law, is obligated to allocate one room — free of charge — to an NSS personnel member. Embarrassing, right? No, I don't like to use harsh words; they can get me arrested. I'd say it is amusing. Now picture this: you own a hotel, and as a business-minded person, you obviously care about your paying customers, right? If today you are informed by the sector commander that they will be stationing one of their regime mascots at your hotel for the next eight months, wouldn't you be kind enough to whisper to your guests, "Be careful what you say, one of them will be here for eight months"? I knew you would. You're not insane, after all. You'd spill the beans, so they'd be careful. You and I know very well that those officers are not there for state interest; they are there for civilian surveillance and unnecessary tension. This shows how broken the security institutions are. They are trained like machines that flag words like "Kiir," "Poor Governance," "Red Belt," "Riek," "Regime Change," "Democracy," and "Bahr el Ghazal." Instead of focusing on making the state safer, they stick around to ensure civilians adjust their conversations, becoming sycophants who praise the regime. All they want are people who say the regime has failed, not even coup plotters. Instead of creating order, they create fear. And fear doesn't always mean obedience; it sometimes means silent rebellion. When security institutions are feared, it means they have failed, because real security institutions rely on trust, information systems, and legal authority.

In a casual chat, an acquaintance once told me how a manager called them privately to warn them: "One of those boys will be here tonight." As it is the norm in Sherkat to discuss government failures openly like football scores, the presence of a spy had to be signaled. As not every single person might know about the presence of a spy, they used local coded language «We are in the company of a hornbill" — just to keep those who talk before they scan the environment from running into trouble. Tell me: what intelligence can you possibly collect when everyone already knows who you are? What secrets can you harvest when your appearance kills every conversation in the room? None!

Now as I wrap up, here are 7 facts about our all-too-mighty NSS.

  1. They are not a functioning national institution, but an extension of regime protection.
  2. We have no real analytical division trained in political science, criminology, cybersecurity, linguistics, regional studies, or forensic psychology. They can't even produce threat assessments, risk models, or long-term reports. Just a few drunk men with no capacity to handle real intelligence.
  3. They don't operate within a legal framework or follow due process. Just a Facebook comment and you'll spend Christmas — if not your entire life — in Blue House.
  4. Intelligence is easily leaked because identities are not concealed, and most of them will tell you everything when they are drunk. Even boda boda riders know the state's secrets, if they should even be called that.
  5. The NSS uses force. True intelligence is subtle. It doesn't get upset or emotional because it carries the fate of the country.
  6. The young men in the NSS crave recognition, and we all know pride comes before a fall.
  7. The NSS is rumor-based, mostly arresting innocent people and missing actual threats.
