Life Loves the Jolly Ones

John GarangJune 4, 2026 · 6 min read
johngarang.com

I am not the oldest person alive, but in the short yet long span of life I have led, I have always noticed how jolly people keep attracting good things into their life, and I'm not talking about politics. That one has a different formula. This is about people who put on a smile even when their world is in flames. People who show up for others, clap for others' wins, laugh loudly, love freely, and don't approach every situation with hate and bitterness.

This group of people keeps moving forward in life. Opportunities find them, the world wants to be around them, doors naturally open for them even when they don't knock, because their personality is inviting, hospitable, and full of good vibes.

But if you compare them to bitter people, there's a big contrast. This group focuses so much on the negatives that they forget to live. Every time someone buys a car, they complain about why someone they bested in class is moving ahead of them in life. Every time someone gets into a fulfilling relationship or gets married, they begin to pull up stories of broken marriages or stereotypes of men and women in relationships. Every time someone graduates, they pull up stories of "a degree is just a piece of paper, and you're not the first to graduate." Every time someone starts a new business, they cite states of entrepreneurial failure in the world. It's always "he/she isn't even that good, he/she got that because of his/her refugee status, scholarships are even for the poor, he/she's just lucky, why is he/she bragging with this ugly partner." Nothing that isn't theirs ever seems right or good.

And here's the dangerous part of bitterness: it blinds you to the beauty of life. Bitter people can't even recognize opportunities because all their energy is vested in monitoring other people's lives instead of fixing their own. They feel someone's downfall as the fulfillment of their prophecy: "You see, I said that business would collapse, I knew she would remain unemployed because that degree was just a paper, told yuh that relationship wouldn't even last a year, and so on!" They try to prove a point by every single misfortune that befalls a trying person. This is all they do, while the whole time, life is passing them by.

A jolly person goes far in life because their heart is light. Even in misfortune, they wake up reset, because their hearts don't carry unnecessary hate. They celebrate others because they know someone's success is not a funeral of their own dreams. They know someone's blessing is a testament that blessings come in turns, and they don't sit complaining about why their time doesn't come. They focus on other things, and "Mungu si adhumani," their blessings always come in bundles and hit them when they least expect it.

Matter of fact, bitter people delay their own blessings because they lock themselves into misery before God looks at them. How can you even pray to God when you're bitter because He elevated someone before you? We were all created in the image of God, and no one wants to give to someone who thinks the world should revolve around them, and so does God.

The atmosphere feels energy. When a hateful person walks into a room, even the windows can feel it. They will always feel like they are rejected and judged, and while most of that is in their hearts and what they project onto a population, it carries weight. Because when people know you for complaints, negativity, bad minds, constant judgement, competing and comparison, they don't want to associate with you. You are not rejected for you; you are rejected by the energy you carry.

Such people rarely keep good friendships. They either stay in friendships where they feed off the other, or in a conniving circle of bitter sorcerers like themselves. Their spirits are always at war. They want to be the most important in rooms, and for that they look beyond what is important: character. They resort to materialism because what they want is not love or admiration; they want people to envy them because, to them, everyone is jealous of someone's growth. If they are not the best dressed person in the room, not the one holding the best smartphone, not the one with the best GPA, or not the most popular, they feel hollow. Because in their minds, if everyone is not envying them, they are not important. They are the kind that will only show up at functions where the talk will be about their attire, their perfume, or their most recent achievement.

But the jolly ones? Life loves them. They will show up in shorts and slippers when everyone is in suits and labeled clothes. They will dance like fools even when everyone sees that as childish or ridiculous, because fulfillment is in their hearts and memories, not in the views of others. They will laugh without effort, support with the little they have, and show good energy. They don't want to project their wealth or intelligence to show supremacy. They become little, even when they are the wealthiest or most popular in the room. These are the people that the world remembers. Their good energy takes their names to positive conversations in their absence. People remember them in times of need and feast.

You don't lose anything when you celebrate people. A friend graduating is something to brag about. It doesn't reduce your intelligence. Your friend getting married doesn't make you a single villain. In fact, you can get free lunch when you visit. Somebody making money doesn't empty your pockets. You even have someone to call when all doors close.

Stop behaving like this life is one plate of rice that everyone has to fight for a scoop in. Mawan even lied in his song. God's breast is not one. They are so many, and you can suckle at your own time. Maybe yours is refilling.

And being miserable isn't being real. It's a popular myth that real people should be cold, rude, spiteful, and isolate themselves. That isn't being real. As a matter of fact, you are only hurting and limiting your true potential thinking that people will think you're a real one. Those who even encourage you to do that are out there trying to mend things with the world. Aura is a feeling that you are being envied when in fact you are foolishly drawing yourself back. You aren't cool; you are exhausting to be around.

The happiest people I know genuinely live life with a purpose. They show up, support their friends' businesses, buy those groundnuts from the kid on the streets even when the world thinks it's childish. They clap for people's achievements, show love publicly, and sleep peacefully at night. When they are offered a drink or meal, they honor the gesture and take it up. They don't fear eating in a gathering because they have never thought that people who eat in gatherings are those who lack food at home. They know the smile of an organizer when everyone's had a good bite, because they know how heavy it feels when you organize something and all the food remains.

At the end of the day, this world has eyes, and it always gives back the energy you put in. If all you throw into the world is hate, bitterness, envy, and negativity, there's no other pot the world is going to scoop your share from. It's going to dip its spoon into that same pot, and your share will taste as bitter as what you put in.

Your personality is not inherent. You shape it by changing your views, by appreciating the little things, by seeing people beyond labels, by seeing yourself as an equal of another human, and by being intentional, not for approval or to be envied but to grow.
