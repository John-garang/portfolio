Courtesy Is Not Weakness: Youth Politics in Bor, Duk and Twic

John GarangFebruary 3, 2026 · 3 min read

Politically, Twic East has produced the most mature youth of this generation. In 2025, Duk, Twic, and Bor all elected youth leaders. All three processes were democratic. No dispute there.

Unfortunately, local democracy did not give them equal treatment after the elections.

One courageous chairman wakes up to a firing squad of critics every day. Every sentence is dissected like the way security agencies examine forensic information. He is the only chairman whose name is dragged through Facebook courts by the foolish, the illiterate, the sorcerers, and the envious. Leaders whose tenures expired seasons ago are trying to come back to relevance using his name.

Of the other two, Mamer sits in the office the way dust exists on furniture. He is present, untouched, and quiet, waiting to be cleaned by the next election. Like Deng Jr, he is invited to speak on most occasions courtesy of his chair, but nothing really lands: no oratory skills, no debate, no friction, and no work loud enough to invite judgement — whether positive or negative. Manyok Jok, on the other hand, has taken a different route. His aspirations are beyond his BCYA tenure, and he has evolved from chairman into KMJ errand boy. He is doing way better than his counterpart, but he tends briefly and disappears quickly, leaving behind not many records worth auditing. Both men are rarely attacked, fairly, because there is little to attack — because there is very little to examine. But even little can be scrutinized and attacked by morally bankrupt fellas, and our youth know better.

And then there is Deng Jr.

His every breath is inspected by individuals who have failed their own spelling tests. Former aspirants and leaders have found ground to regain relevance. As the most engaged youth leader, he is the target of political retirees who have refused burial and are trying to resuscitate the relevance they failed to achieve during their era. One of those men had nothing but a WhatsApp group handover to show for the end of his era. The other had only the reckless Jieng-Jonglei secession Gumbo speech as his only leadership achievement. And there is the quick-to-rant, quick-to-apologize, bitter owner of the bat-infested closed clinic, whose only achievement, besides a failed political career, is "I have never gone to court with your daughters."

These three men should attend the school of thought of former TEYA aspirants and leaders. Leaders like Garang Atem Ayiik, Ajang Diing, and Arok Dut Arok: when their time passed, they went home. They knew they had taken their chance. They did not haunt the successors of the neighbor communities like unpaid debts. They are quiet, and you will rarely see their names pop up criticizing any of the three communities. Former aspirants like Dut Atem Malek, Isaac Khot, Adier Simon, etcetera, accepted their time had not come and waited. They are quiet, waiting for the next election to make their point. As for Khot, he has moved on and is now waiting for when he will compete for TECA chairmanship. That is exemplary leadership.

All three chairmen are human and they err. Twic East youth understand this. That is why, no matter how empty Mamer sounds or how directionless Manyok behaves, you will never see Twic youth marching to expose their shallowness. That silence is not weakness. It is courtesy. It is respect for sisterly communities.

But courtesy here is one-directional — never reciprocated.

They attack our chairman daily and still expect us to disarm. They insult him and demand our restraint. They provoke and then preach peace. That hypocrisy is loud.

If a fair judge were to study youth politics across Bor, Duk, and Twic, the conclusion would be uncomfortable but obvious: Twic stands taller. Not because her leaders or youth are perfect, but because they are trained in empathy and respect for the neighbors. Because her youth understand coexistence better than online warfare.

Garang Mabior did not come from Twic by accident.

I believe in destiny, but I do not believe in its mystical nature; I believe it has habits. And I'm certain Mabior Garang is not a reincarnation of his father. But if history ever repeats itself in flesh, it will not rise from comfort. It will rise from the East. It will rise hated by outsiders, resisted by neighbors, doubted by elders, and still grow into one of you again. Celebrated later. Loved late. Claimed after sunset.

That is how it always goes.

Twic East is not loud because it lacks leaders. It is loud because it has one.
