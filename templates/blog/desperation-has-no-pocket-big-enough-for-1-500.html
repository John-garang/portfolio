Desperation Has No Pocket Big Enough for $1,500

John GarangKigali · December 7, 2025 · 3 min read
johngarang.com

Last night I received news of the passing of a childhood friend in Libya, and I had to leave this here!

If you can raise $1,500 for the Libya route, you're not "desperate" or "escaping poverty." You're chasing a fantasy. The term "desperate journey" has long been misused, but I tell you, "desperation has no pocket big enough to hide $1,500." Your motive for that journey is not from a pain point; it's greed wearing the mask of survival.

I've seen my age-mates, and even those younger than me, saving up for Libya. No war chased them, hunger didn't drag them by the collar — they made enough to get by. But because someone shared a photo on Instagram in Athens, Stockholm, or Prague, and because you graduated from high school together, you believe Europe is only a desert away, quickly forgetting that desert has swallowed hundreds of thousands of hopefuls. We imagine futures that don't belong to us, and in that fantasy, we pretend not to see the numbers Libya has already swallowed. Truth is, the Libya route is now a coin we all toss for our fate. Heads, you survive and get your life in Europe (it's not always as you imagine, but that's a story for another day); tails, your family has to find out about your demise online, and your dying mother will have to pray over a body she'll never see again. Worst case scenario, your family will conduct funeral prayers for the unknown once a year of silence is gone.

It surprises me, almost drives me mad, how one leaves a roof over their head, a filled plate, and a gig that gave him the money he'll give to smugglers, and still ends up in a car trunk as they look for an owner for you — by the way, you become a commodity then. How do you call that desperation? No, I'm not buying that. That's not desperation, that's greed. It's a rare kind of greed that brainwashes you to believe slavery doesn't exist anymore. But yes, I guess you hate yourself once you become a slave in the 21st century. Only then does your greed condense and you realize waking up a free man to go get that $100/month is worth more than life in Europe in ways your spirit cannot gather.

Wanting more than the land that raised us, more than the work that feeds us, more than the jobs that earn us the $1,500 passage fee, is a greedy, fast version of success. It's a quick fantasy future of all the pictures you feed on online and the warnings you ignore, without the patience behind it.

But being beaten in a warehouse and moved around like a commodity isn't success. Begging for a drop of water isn't struggle. There's no success in being negotiated and bought by the hour. It's the 21st century; slavery should have ended, or so it is claimed. But despite all this, you still leave, you still risk, and this foolishness — this foolishness you call dreaming big?

If truly you had nothing, and you were desperate, you wouldn't afford the journey. Perhaps this journey doesn't start in despair. Maybe, just maybe, you stood before the wrong mirror, where "I have something" was reflected as "I have not enough." If wisdom was a piece of cake for all of us, maybe we'd realize that what's killing us isn't the desert, it's not the Mediterranean, it's not the smugglers, neither is it SKM. It's the hunger to be rich overnight in a world and time that only pays in years.
