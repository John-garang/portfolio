2025: The Year of Politics, Shenanigans, and Professional Nonsense

John GarangNimule · December 30, 2025 · 5 min read

2025 has been riddled and generous with political failure, moral collapse, elite stupidity, men discovering too late that power has an expiry date, a few redemptions, and food-for-thought hurdles and scandals!

By popular vote, the Bol Mel circus comes first.

The man who sprinted at the sight of the finish line, celebrated before the first lap was done, and fell before seeing what lay beyond. A man with a brief and inspirational rise and a permanent and educational fall. Not only did his ambition deflate, but he was also cut down by the highest authority, and reduced to rickshaw-level pro-Greater Bor mony Twic in Jebel Lemon, sweating loudly for relevance and external validation.

The Nasir Saga.

The White Army's deadliest chapter in 9 years. A tragedy that swallowed Lt. Gen. David Majur Dak, White Army leader Tor Gile Thoan Meen, 245 servicemen, over 500 civilians, and an uncounted number of militia casualties. May the dead rest (peace is for God to decide). But don't worry, accountability survived… by dying quietly. The aftermath was classic SPLM theatre, with retaliatory airstrikes from Kiir — blame dumped neatly on private Bol Mel Kuol (then everything at once: General, Vice President, SPLM First Deputy, and a convenient scapegoat). Meanwhile, First Vice President Dr. Riek Machar and co. sit in court for treason charges that live in eternal adjournment. Meanwhile, our deadbeat VP for the Youth and Gender Cluster is picking her office title from the ground like loose change, dusting herself off, trying to locate the job description on the floor. Maybe she will get this one right!

The Nyolo Massacre.

Dozens of Bor pastoralists slaughtered. Zero accountability, and a daylight cinema of the incompetence and uselessness of Jonglei's political and military leaders. Not failing, useless! If silence, confusion, and uselessness were skills, they would be professors. Kuol Manyang was there in Nairobi, smiling for the cameramen — just long enough to show he tried before carrying out Kiir's orders of "No Peace Agreement, we must eat alone!" While Lomuru cried over his political crops.

The Red Belt Delusion and the Reincarnation of Malueth Aguto.

Buor paying the price for the stupid chant "We have never rebelled." A population punished for loyalty to a greedy, deaf government. Bor's unpopular commissioner, who by all traits and background resembles a child sired by Malueth Aguto, and his loose tongue that opens before his empty brain wakes up from money sickness. A picture-perfect translation that a loose tongue attached to a dull brain is a dangerous thing.

Gen. Magok Magok and John Kon: A Sitcom.

The Warrap governor who mastered the art of decree addiction from Kiir and outperformed his master by improving the formula: releasing them every week like Premier League games. John Kon turned traffic laws into monthly comedy episodes, while screaming at the IGP's decrees tantrumously, because he's the SPLM CEO's home boy. He taught us rules are optional when you know the owner, and just yesterday he was promoted to Lt. General.

Waat Press' 7th Most Useless Official of 2025.

Riek Gai Kok and his newly acquired fine art of Payam and Bomapreneurship. Less than a year in power, and he has sold lands and auctioned borders. His protégé, or should I say it politely because he is my commissioner, Juach Arok, has learned from the best and is now making a fortune from monetizing Bomas in Twic East. Governance is now a side hustle.

Deng Jr and Jonglei Youth Leadership.

The man loved by many. Hated by many. Opposed by his own. Not so national, but it's my article, not democracy. If you disagree, write your own. Junior shook the internet, but yes, you can't serve two masters, and so he's not for everyone. We, his own, love him the way he is! In Elgeyo-Marakwet county they say, "Ruto Two Term"; in Twic East County we say, "Deng Jr Two Term!" Go hang yourselves 🙄

Optimism and Tearspreneurship.

Agar Madong's two sons. One doing his PhD on Facebook, and his protégé, a crybaby making, or at least attempting to make, a fortune on tears. He was crying last night, calling on Bahr el Ghazal to help him fight Buor, who his intoxication convinced him had beaten him. One of his relatives, who has 10x the brain size of that duck, tried to justify the video as a past occurrence. (We're not that dumb; metadata can clear that cover-up. Makorou is a journalist; he should know a thing or two about digital verification tools.) Someone translated this Swahili phrase for him: "Pombe si Uji!"

The Bigger Picture.

I will close with something far broader. This year exposed a quiet rebellion. Former diners at the big table of pork, luxury, oil revenues, and imported wines were stripped of serving spoons and handed forks. They could not scoop at the same pace and magnitude as the SPLM CEO and Executive Director, Senior Presidential Envoy on Special Programs Adut, their family, and inner circle. Hunger creates ideology fast and produces rebels faster than oppression.

Now everyone is a rebel based in London, Nairobi, Pretoria, etcetera. Funny, right? They want us, the youth, to repeat our fathers' mistakes. Join loose alliances. Die. Then watch them, like our president, resume business as usual, like it was after secession from Khartoum. If any rebel leader wants true comradeship, bring your sons to the bush, and start the first attack, not on the Nimule-Juba Road; go to Akoon where Awan Chan Nyiel rests. If you give that signal, you may trigger our interest. The Warrior of Good Cause, a nickname given to the late chairman Dr. John Garang, wasn't given when he was in Washington, or bought. He earned it just outside the same bushes today's militias roam, where bullets are real, not rhetorical.

Any youth dumb enough to fall for recycled rebellion is a big fool and deserves a lesson they will learn the hard way. (Blue House)

The only man worth reconsidering is Peter Biar Ajak. A man, although flawed in many ways, emotionally blunt and reckless, and abusive with words, who tried to revive the liberation with bullets, not press statements and WhatsApp groups (he had one, that he emotionally removed me from, but haidhuru). His TikTok rants may be contradictory, but his last dance was an effort towards what must really be done to free this country. I pray his efforts of heroism don't turn him into a Nelson Mandela that will not be redeemed.

As Garang said, the blood of our people must be spilled, and so it must be. Any rebel leader must know that diplomacy and elections are past J-awan; I won't even talk of press releases from London or whatever city. Only blood will bring the long-sought freedom. But that blood must be spilled under the command of a true warrior of good cause, not someone who is not satisfied with a fork in a room where others are using serving spoons!

Lastly, if you're still asking who Majak D'Agoot was in the history of South Sudan, here's the answer:

Majak D'Agoot ee mony ë mor 🫩
